## Voice / TTS

### KittenTTS 2 (KittenML / Stellon Labs) -- TEST
- Link: https://huggingface.co/KittenML/kitten-tts-2 (updated 2026-10-23; first seen here). Code: https://github.com/KittenML/kitten-tts-2-cpp
- What: 1.7B speech LM writing S3 codec tokens, 24 kHz output. In-context voice cloning from 5-30 s of one speaker; 115 built-in voices; language is chosen by voice. Beta emotion tags ([joyful] etc.) and vocal events (<laugh>, <sigh>, <gasp>). Per the model card.
- Languages (card): ar, zh, nl, fr, de, hi, id, it, ja, ko, pl, pt, ru, es, tr, en. That covers ALL eight of OTR's languages (en, es, fr, it, pt, ja, zh, hi), which Ming did not. Non-English text needs normalize=False.
- OTR lane: sits beside Kokoro; adds cloning and per-character voices with real expression tags.
- Fit: card says weights are 947 MiB (ternary-packed LM 910 MiB + 37 MiB decoder; bf16 full is 3.47 GB; 4-bit embedding variant 469 MB, lossy). Runs on CPU by default, and the authors' llama.cpp fork is the fast path. No VRAM or real-time-factor number published (not measured by anyone I could find). Estimate: well under 8 GB on GPU, so both boxes.
- ComfyUI requirement: no core support and I found no node pack for it (registry search for tts/voice showed none). It would need a thin wrapper node around the `kittenml` pip package (Python 3.10+, torch). audio.cpp (https://huggingface.co/audio-cpp/audio.cpp-gguf) lists kitten-tts-2 among its GGUF conversions -- not checked in depth.
- Licence: Stellon Labs Community License (free research / non-commercial / limited commercial; ends past USD 1M revenue or funding; attribution required). Not a gate. Weights ungated.
- Risk: new and unmeasured; ternary 1.7B may sound thinner than the bf16; emotion tags are marked Beta; speed unknown. The "ear test" is the real question (period radio delivery).
- Verdict: TEST -- probe on CPU first for real-time factor, then clone one voice and compare against Kokoro in 3 languages.

## Video

### MiniMax H3 acceleration: FastH3-QAD-v2 (NVFP4) and PTD+RefAsymOPD LoRAs -- WATCH
- FastH3-QAD-v2: https://huggingface.co/FastVideo/FastH3-QAD-v2 (updated 2026-10-19). Card read. 8-step video+audio model, NVFP4 W4A4 transformer, block-sparse VSA attention, "runs on one Blackwell GPU (RTX 5090, RTX PRO 6000) or DGX Spark". nvfp4 transformer file is 11.9 GB, text encoder ~16.5 GB (NVFP4 Qwen3-VL listed), VAE int8 2.1 GB. Published quality metric is error against the bf16 teacher (relative L2 0.38 video, 0.32 audio, measured by the authors on 30 prompts) -- not a perceptual score. Licence: "other". Runs via the FastVideo library, not a ComfyUI core loader that I could confirm.
- PTD+RefAsymOPD LoRAs: https://huggingface.co/taobao-content-ai/MiniMax-H3-PTD-RefAsymOPD-LoRAs (updated 2026-10-26, card read). 8-step and 6-step Ref2VA per-hop LoRAs (rank 128) on MiniMax-H3, claim better background and sharpness than plain PTD. Card says the ComfyUI nodes are the same code as plain PTD. Compare is vs. a 40-step teacher, by the authors. The shipped lane is h3_low_audio_in / ref2va, so this is the one that would challenge it.
- 16 GB fit: 5080 is Blackwell (NVFP4 capable), but 16 GB vs the 5090's 32 GB is the open question; no 16 GB number published. The shipped H3 lanes already need about 39-40 GB of downloads (apple/VIDEO_MODELS.md).
- Operator rule: no quality-for-speed trades, so any test is an eye/ear A/B against the shipped steps.
- Verdict: WATCH (both; the LoRAs are the cheaper test once their ComfyUI node route is confirmed).
