## 1. WHAT OTR IS AND WHAT AN EPISODE LOOKS LIKE

OTR (ComfyUI-OldTimeRadio) is a fully local, offline-first ComfyUI custom-node pack that produces old-time-radio episodes as video. One run of a single canonical graph does everything: a local LLM writes a script into a production LEDGER (a JSON document of cast, shots, beats, lines, timing and receipts); a TTS engine (Kokoro, Bark, IndexTTS2 by profile) voices every line and the lines are placed on a master mix; an image engine mints portraits and per-beat stills when the chosen video engine asks for them; a video engine renders one clip per beat; a CPU composite lays the clips on the audio timeline, burns SDH captions, appends a credits roll, muxes the master audio and publishes the final MP4 to `otr/obs/`. The writer LLM is `google/gemma-4-E2B-it` on the 8 GB floor AND on the dev-box episode cited in section 2 (its ledger stamps `creative_writing_model = technical_model = google/gemma-4-E2B-it`, `llm_policy.vram_ceiling_gb = 6.8`); the 16 GB machine-class row and the 5080 overnight profile `otr_5080_haunted_12b_overnight` use `google/gemma-4-12b-it` with `quant_policy = bnb_nf4` and a 14.5 GB writer ceiling. A typical episode is 1.5 to 3.5 minutes, 8 to 12 beats, one act; the 12B overnight loops run 4-5 acts (28-37 clips). Two operator rules govern everything that follows. First, **publication to `otr/obs/` is the success signal**: a leg that does not put an episode there did not pass, however green its logs, and a leg that has run more than five minutes with nothing in `obs` is treated as failing. Second, **judge it as radio drama with pictures**: the operator reads outcomes by eye, "overall better" is the verdict, and no technical rubric outranks what he sees on screen.

## 5B. THE VISUAL STYLE -- what it is, how it is supposed to reach the picture, and why the AnimateDiff lane lost it

**The operator's words:** "the prompting for this new AnimateDiff workflow must obey the visual
style; what we have now is usually not doing anime or other styles anymore; I think we lost the
visual style." Everything below was read from the real files on 2026-09-02 (provenance in
parentheses); the conclusion is that he is right, and the mechanism is exact.

### What a visual style IS in OTR

There are two things called "style" and only one of them is visual. `meta.style` is the radio
grammar (a 100-entry catalog of sound-world / story-engine / ending-mode combinations such as
`locked_room_suspense`); it shapes the story, not the look, and it "lied on screen twice in one day"
until the on-screen treatment was pointed at the visual field instead. The VISUAL style is a flat
JSON pack, one file per style, ten of them: `anime`, `archival_documentary`, `cartoon`,
`paper_origami`, `recur_frac`, `sci_fi_radio` (the default), `shakespeare_stage_realism`,
`storybook_engraving`, `video_art`, plus a tenth dynamic style, `visual_storybased`, composed at
run time from a nine-field "Visual Style Card" the writer LLM authors for that story
(nodes/visual_styles/*.json; nodes/_otr_visual_styles.py:71-136, :272-347).

A pack carries prompt LANGUAGE only. Its fields: `positive_tail`, `image_grade_tail`,
`broadcast_tail`, `era_tail`, `negative_tail`; eleven "look" strings (`portrait_look`,
`portrait_look_talking`, `portrait_instruction_look`, `scene_instruction_look`,
`announcer_subject_face`, `announcer_subject_ltx_mouth`, `announcer_subject_object`,
`radio_object_look`, `plate_look`, `non_character_emblem_fallback`, `still_word_title_mood_style`);
and four dictionaries (`open_subjects`, `motion_registers` for the announcer and the three music
slots, `still_word_typography`, `still_word_backdrop`). **There is no checkpoint, no LoRA, no
per-engine field in the schema.** The writer's own tooltip says it: the style "rewrites ONLY the
downstream still/video prompt style language (tails)". Two packs, verbatim and trimmed:

* `anime`: positive tail "anime style, expressive linework, cel-shaded color"; portrait look
  "hand-drawn anime character art, clean expressive linework, cel-shaded color, dramatic rim
  light"; scene look "hand-drawn cel-shaded anime scene with expressive linework and a painterly
  background"; negative "photograph, photorealistic, realistic skin pores, film grain, 3d render,
  muddy color, text, watermark"; announcer motion register "Continuous shot, same console
  throughout. Dial needle sweeps in crisp arcs. Speed lines flare and fade. Cel highlights shiver
  across the cabinet. Slow dramatic push-in."
* `sci_fi_radio` (the default): positive tail "cinematic, 35mm film look, subtle film grain,
  volumetric lighting"; grade tail "anamorphic lens, heavy vignette, muted color grade, sharp
  focus"; scene look empty; negative ends "... cartoon, illustration, text, watermark".

### The knob and the roll

The style is chosen on the writer node (`OTR_LedgerScriptWriter`, widget `visual_style`; default
`sci_fi_radio`). The shipped graph carries the sentinel `"roll (any style)"`, so every episode
rolls one of the ten with equal odds from OS entropy (or from `OTR_VISUAL_STYLE_SEED` when pinned),
and the receipt lands in the ledger as `meta.visual_style` (the id) and `meta.style_roll`
(requested, selected, seed, seed source, eligible order). A dynamic pack is embedded in the ledger
with a sha256 receipt re-verified on every read. The operator ruled "randomized is fine"
(rulings, 2026-08-04). Credits and the on-screen treatment print `meta.visual_style`.

### Style to STILLS -- this half works

Every portrait and scene still is composed with the pack's language: the portrait anchor is
"<geometry>, <portrait_look>"; scene stills are "subject / setting / framing / era tail /
positive tail / grade tail / broadcast tail / no-text clause"; the LLM-written character scenes
carry `portrait_instruction_look` and `scene_instruction_look` and are finished with the era and
positive tails; plates use `plate_look`. The image dispatcher then front-anchors a compact style
cue on every still and composes the pack's negative. Engines differ in how much of that they can
honour: Z-Image-Turbo ships at cfg 1.0, where the negative is inert and the positive tail carries
the look (a ruling of 2026-09-01: the old cfg 2.0, kept only to make the negative live, gave the
"red-wash" skin); FLUX.2 Klein wants the style token APPENDED, not prepended ("style-first makes
style dominate while the subject drifts"), yet the dispatcher prepends for every engine (the
per-engine notes are "stored, not wired"); Lumina keeps a live negative. A live receipt from this
morning (`frosts_embrace_20260902_072435`, `archival_documentary`, Klein) shows every still row
`styled: true`, `negative_source: pack`.

### Style to VIDEO on the AnimateDiff lane -- this half is where it was lost

The AnimateDiff lane is `animatediff15_v3_haunted_video` (the v3 motion module plus the v3 adapter
LoRA on a Stable Diffusion 1.5 base; the five older AnimateDiff siblings are retired). Its contract:
`accepts_still = False`, `still_plan = ()`, `subject_ownership = "prompt"`; cfg 8.0, 20 steps, the
negative prompt LIVE. Its prompt is authored per beat by the ShotLock pass (a "leaf" the local LLM
writes under rules that never mention the medium, and a validator that REJECTS any leaf carrying
"style", "photorealistic" or "cinematic") and finalized at render time as:

    positive = <compact style cue> . <motif>, <leaf>, <mode law>
    negative = <lane hygiene> + <pack negative_tail>

where the compact style cue is the FIRST TWO WORDS of the pack's positive tail (or up to the word
"style" within the first four). Computed live from the packs: `anime` gives "anime style", `cartoon`
"bright cartoon", `storybook_engraving` "storybook engraving", `paper_origami` "folded paper",
`shakespeare_stage_realism` "photorealistic Shakespearean", `archival_documentary` "archival
documentary", `recur_frac` "recursive fractal light field", and `sci_fi_radio` gives NOTHING (its
tail starts "cinematic, 35mm ..."). The dynamic style degrades worst: a card whose medium reads
"Charcoal and graphite on textured heavy-weight paper" becomes the cue "Charcoal and".

So on this lane the whole pack contributes exactly two things: that cue and the negative. The
composer's own docstring says it "never reads a pack motion register, an open subject or a motion
clause"; `portrait_look`, `scene_instruction_look`, `plate_look`, the announcer subjects, the four
motion registers and the era and grade tails are all dropped. This was recorded on 2026-08-22 as
"the Ghost prompt emits a 21-char style cue where the pack authors 262" and marked WONTFIX at the
time (the anime v2 episode had measured far worse temporal stability than v3, so the cue was kept
short on purpose). A code comment from the same week records the mechanism from the other side: the
word "real" in a mode law "rendered photographs of rooms and people -- 8 of 8 legible and not one
frame that looked anime. The pack cue owns the medium."

Then the three things that would make a style physically possible on SD1.5 are all absent:

* **(a) the prompt** carries the style as a two-word prefix only (present in every one of the
  1,310 Ghost prompts tallied across the two overnight logs: 242 "photorealistic Shakespearean",
  238 "bright cartoon", 194 "archival documentary", 168 "folded paper", 112 "storybook engraving",
  109 "anime style", 82 "Charcoal and", 56 "recursive fractal");
* **(b) the checkpoint** is one fixed file for every style, `v1-5-pruned-emaonly-fp16.safetensors`
  (base SD1.5, no anime, no cartoon, no engraving checkpoint; no override knob);
* **(c) the LoRA** is the same `v3_sd15_adapter.ckpt` at strength 1.0 on every style, and the
  engine's own comment documents that adapter as re-adding "the video training set's own
  defects ... 1.0 is the full video-dataset domain" -- a photographic-grime domain adapter under
  an "anime style" prompt;
* **(d) the conditioning still** does not exist on this lane (`accepts_still = False`; the
  portrait-free roles policy), so the styled stills that DO exist for the same beat never reach
  the video.

The measured result matches the operator's eye: every recent AnimateDiff episode records its style
in the ledger (`the_weight_of_the_ink_20260830_195508` rolled `anime`; two 08-31 episodes rolled
`shakespeare_stage_realism`; today's `the_tectal_echo` rolled `storybook_engraving`), every video
prompt begins with the two-word cue, and the frames come out as base-SD1.5 photographic grime with
a hint of the word. Nothing strips the style afterwards: the damping-word audit is about motion
adjectives, and the prop substitution route only swaps props. The style is not being removed; it
is being asked for with two words and no tools.

### How the style COULD interact with stills and video (open-ended, on purpose; these are experiment arms E10-E14 in section 7)

The operator asked for this to stay open: the goal is that the AnimateDiff workflow OBEYS the
visual style, and that the style, the stills and the video agree. The facts above leave several
doors, and which one drives the new lane is the question, not a decision:

1. **Let the still carry the style into the video.** The still lane already renders every beat in
   the pack's full language on an engine that honours it. AnimateDiff-Evolved's SparseCtrl (RGB
   keyframes) or an IP-Adapter image prompt can take that styled still as the anchor of the clip,
   so the medium comes from a picture rather than from two words. This is the only door that also
   solves identity and setting continuity across the beat. Cost: SparseCtrl / IP-Adapter model
   files (check presence in the capability inventory), a still per video beat (already produced
   for most roles), and the lane's contract flipping to `accepts_still = True`.
2. **Give the style its own tools on the video lane.** A per-style SD1.5 checkpoint (an anime
   checkpoint for `anime`, a toon checkpoint for `cartoon`, an ink or engraving model for
   `storybook_engraving`) and, where one exists, a style LoRA, chosen from the pack id at render
   time, with the v3 adapter strength lowered for non-photographic styles. The pack schema would
   gain optional `sd15_checkpoint` / `sd15_loras` / `adapter_strength` fields, empty for packs
   that do not care. This is the door that changes the most about the recipe and must be A/A-nulled
   against the current cells before it ships anywhere.
3. **Let the pack's language back into the prompt, deliberately.** Send more than two words: the
   pack's `scene_instruction_look` (or a new `video_look` field) as the medium clause, and the
   pack's motion register for the announcer and music beats, while keeping the leaf medium-free.
   The 08-22 stability finding is why the cue is short today; the honest way through is a
   same-seed A/A pair per style measuring what the longer clause costs in flicker before it is kept.
4. **Make the roll style-aware for this lane.** Not every style is realistic for SD1.5 video; the
   roll could weight or exclude packs per lane (the machine-class saved dropdowns already carry
   per-lane choices), so an `anime` roll lands on a lane that can draw anime.
5. **Persist the final video prompt in the ledger.** Today only a hash and a 100-character log line
   survive, so nobody can audit what a clip was asked for after the fact. Cheap, and a precondition
   for judging any of the doors above with the operator's eye rather than from logs.

Whichever door is chosen, the acceptance test is the same: pick `anime`, render the same ledger and
seed through the current lane and the changed lane, and put the two clips in front of the operator
(the A/A null first). "Looks like anime" is the verdict; the stills and the video must look like
the same show; and the name, the gender, the portrait and the voice must agree, which is the
correctness rule that already stands.

## 5C. PROMPTING ANIMATEDIFF V3 WITH THE ADAPTER FOR BEST EFFECT -- an open research question

The operator asked for this to be part of the statement: "maybe part of the problem is properly
how to prompt AnimateDiff v3 with the adapter for best effect; that can be in the problem
statement for research as well, if we do not already have it." We do not have it as a settled
recipe. What is KNOWN, so a researcher starts from facts and not from a blank page:

* **The prompt shape today** is `<two-word style cue>. <motif>, <leaf>, <mode law>` on the
  positive side and `<lane hygiene> + <pack negative>` on the negative side, at cfg 8.0 and 20
  steps with the negative live. The leaf is written by the local LLM per beat under rules that
  forbid any medium word, so the medium can only come from the cue. The motif and the mode law are
  fixed authored fragments (the lane's own "Ghost" grammar).
* **Two measured facts fence it.** Longer, richer style clauses were tried on this lane's
  predecessor and cost temporal stability (the 2026-08-22 finding that kept the cue short); and
  "damping words" (subtle, restrained, stable, gentle and their kin) are banned from every motion
  prompt because they were measured to flatten the motion the operator wants (PBUG-20260827-04).
  The 2026-08-22 note that one word, "real", turned eight of eight anime frames photographic shows
  how strongly single tokens steer SD1.5 video.
* **The adapter is a domain, not a style.** The v3 adapter LoRA at 1.0 puts the clip fully inside
  the video-training domain (documented in the engine as re-adding that dataset's defects and
  grime); its strength is the one knob nobody has swept per style.
* **Nothing about the prompt is persisted** except a hash and a 100-character log line, so past
  episodes cannot be re-read for what worked; that has to be fixed before any prompt study can be
  judged by eye against the ledger.

What the research should answer, each with a citation or a measured A/A pair, never an opinion:

1. The prompt grammar SD1.5 + AnimateDiff v3 actually rewards: order (medium first or last),
   length, quality tags, weighting syntax, whether a comma list or a sentence reads better, and
   how many motion words a 16-frame window can honour before it flickers.
2. What the v3 adapter strength does across 0.4-1.0 on a non-photographic style, and whether a
   per-style strength is the cheapest way to let anime and engraving through.
3. How SparseCtrl (RGB keyframes from the beat's styled still) and IP-Adapter change what the prompt
   needs to say: when the picture carries the medium, does the prompt drop to motion only?
4. Whether motion LoRAs (pan, tilt, zoom, roll) and the pack's motion registers should replace the
   authored mode law for camera language, and how they interact with the leaf.
5. Whether prompt scheduling / prompt travel across the context windows (one clause per window)
   gives a beat more life than one static prompt, at what stability cost.
6. Negative-prompt practice at cfg 8: what belongs in the lane hygiene list, what belongs to the
   pack, and whether the pack negative should grow per style (the anime pack already bans
   "photograph, photorealistic, film grain, 3d render").

Any answer that changes the shipped cells goes through the same-seed A/A null and the operator's
eye before it is kept (rulings: recipes are hard-won; flicker is a recipe problem; judge it as
radio drama).

## 6. THE PROBLEM STATEMENT

We ship AnimateDiff v3 with its domain adapter at an unmeasured strength, but we drive it with a prompt of which only one 96-char leaf is authored and an empty latent, and we ignore the two things that make each episode its own: a ledger that already knows the setting (three times over), the palette, the lighting, the key objects, the grouping of lines into shots, the exact speech timing and energy of every line, and each character's description and voice -- and an image phase that can mint a portrait-consistent 1472x832 still for every beat but is switched off for this lane. The question, in the operator's framing: **are we taking advantage of what AnimateDiff v3 can do, and how do the ledger and per-beat stills make each episode's video unique and more alive -- identity and setting persisting across a beat, motion that follows the drama -- rather than eleven unrelated 320-character hallucinations -- and how does the new lane OBEY THE VISUAL STYLE, which today reaches the video as two words on a fixed photographic base (section 5B)?** The operator's own ordering of the first experiments: putting the per-beat STILLS into the video is one of the first one to three arms to try (E1, E2 and E7 below all do it, and the still is also how the style can travel); the rest follow. Constraints: 16 GB VRAM with a 14.5 GB real target on the dev box (the haunted phase already peaks at 13.3-14.2 GB on the 12B overnight ledgers and 13.5 GB on the E2B episode, with the writer unloaded first -- so the headroom below 14.5 GB is 300 MB to 1.2 GB, not several GB) and 8 GB on the proven floor; SD1.5-class base at 512x288; sequential execution only; fully offline, $0, no cloud; one canonical graph with per-machine saved dropdowns, no per-machine JSONs; no story-quality work (prose is accepted as is); no damping words; and the recipe's proven cells (v3 module + adapter, 20/8.0/euler/normal, 512x288 hold-2, static 16/4 pyramid, the negative, the Gen1 graph shape) are not to be degraded -- every experiment is an ADDITION judged against the current recipe, not a replacement of it, and a Gen2 arm is a REPLACEMENT that must be named as such.

## 7. EXPERIMENT DIRECTIONS THE FACTS SUPPORT

Ranked by expected on-screen payoff per unit of risk. Every experiment shares the same judging protocol: render the current recipe twice on the same seed and same ledger (the A/A null -- what "no change" looks like, since even a fixed seed is not bit-stable across context windows), then render the experiment on that seed, publish all three to `otr/obs/`, and let the operator's eye decide. Same seed is guaranteed by section 2's seed row: re-running the same frozen ledger (same brief terms, same cast rows, same beat/char ids) through a changed graph or engine reproduces every `render_request_hash` and therefore every seed. A regression on a proven cell (a 4060 8 GB leg failing to publish, or the peak crossing 14.5 GB on the 5080) is a fail regardless of how the picture looks.

**E0 (before anything else): the adapter strength sweep.** The shipped 1.0 is unqualified (section 2). Sweep `OTR_GHOST_HAUNTED_LORA_STRENGTH` at 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0 on the A/A seed, publish, and freeze the number in `ADAPTER_V3_STRENGTH`. Zero code, zero downloads, and it decides what "the current recipe" even looks like before anything is layered on it.

**Prerequisite for E1, E2, E5, E7 (one code change, shared):** the haunted engine declares `accepts_still = True`, `required_inputs = ("text_prompt", "init_image")`, and a `still_plan` with a `scene_beat` row (`per_beat`, `wide`, `always`, plus `scene_character` for character beats -- `scene_open` for the opening bookend if the bookend should get one too). The existing dispatcher then mints 1472x832 stills into `episodes/<ep>/stills/`, stamps `images.images[]`, the identity seed ties character stills to the portrait, and `_still_index` hands the path to the engine as it already does for LTX; the engine must downscale 1472x832 -> 512x288 (2.875x) itself. The image phase runs as node 91 before the video phase (node 92), so their VRAM peaks do not stack. **On the 8 GB class this is a real cost:** the lane is in `_NO_STILL_VIDEO_ENGINES` exactly so the 4060 is not gated on the ~11 GB Klein bundle (`otr_provision.py:1543`); declaring `init_image` reverses that, Klein gets downloaded and gated, and `docs/ship-audit-2026-09-01/4060_CLEANROOM.md` shows what Klein costs on 8 GB: ~20-21 s a still (1.07 s/step x 20 steps) ONLY after the writer LLM is freed before the first still (`da2b7a36`), and ~120-143 s PER STEP (~42 min a still, "WORKS, UNUSABLE") under stock flags when the writer was still resident. No 8 GB run has yet combined Klein stills with an AnimateDiff render in one episode, so the 4060 leg of any still-fed experiment is itself a first. Cost: one still per beat, plus the image weights on a box that does not already have them.

1. **Still-seeded latent (img2img init).** Uses: the per-beat `scene_beat`/`scene_character` still (identity-seeded from the portrait), `cast[].character_description`, `shots[].description`. Change: replace `EmptyLatentImage` with `LoadImage -> resize to 512x288 -> VAEEncode -> RepeatLatentBatch(U)` and lower denoise to a sweep of 0.6 / 0.75 / 0.9 (denoise is the only recipe cell touched, and only on this arm). On screen: every frame of the beat starts from the same room and the same face, so setting and identity persist for the beat; the adapter's "degraded transmission" texture is laid over a coherent picture instead of a fresh hallucination. Cost: zero downloads, zero new packs, roughly the same sampling time, VRAM unchanged. Risk: too low a denoise gives a "breathing still" (a repeated identical init damps motion); mitigated by keeping cfg/steps and by E4's additive motion clauses. Judge: A/A null, then the three denoise arms.

2. **Noised image injection at a mid-point.** Uses the same still, but through the chain `LoadImage -> ADE_NoisedImageInjection(image, vae from the checkpoint loader, resize_image=True, start_percent, strength_multival from ADE_MultivalDynamic) -> IMAGE_INJECT -> ADE_AnimateDiffSamplingSettings.image_inject -> SAMPLE_SETTINGS -> ADE_AnimateDiffLoaderGen1.sample_settings`, leaving `EmptyLatentImage` and denoise 1.0 intact. On screen: the still is pulled into the latent partway through sampling, so early steps keep the motion freedom of the shipped recipe and later steps snap to the room and face. Cost: zero downloads; the pinned-empty `sample_settings` socket goes from empty to a three-node chain (sampling settings, injection, multival). Risk: ADE injection behaviour under the domain adapter is not established; sweep `start_percent` 0.0/0.3/0.6 and strength 0.5/1.0. Judge as E1. E1 vs E2 is itself the interesting A/B.

3. **Ledger-driven prompt scheduling inside the beat.** Uses `ADE_PromptScheduling` with frame-indexed keyframes and the fields Prompt v2 currently ignores: `shots[].description` / `scenes[].description` / `episode_canon.setting` (setting), `meta.visual_palette`, `story_brief_terms.lighting`, `key_objects`, `episode_canon.time_of_day`, and on writer banks `traits`/`beat_intent`/`arc_phase`; keyframe positions from `lines[].dur_s` and `word_count` (open on the setting + motif, land on the leaf's action by the last third). **Scope note:** all of that new text is DETERMINISTIC Python -- the ledger fields are already strings, and the only LLM-authored slot stays the existing `drawable_beat` leaf, so E3 needs no new authoring pass, no new hash key and no reauthoring; it is a second composer beside `compose_ghost_prompt_v2` (or a keyframe list built from its components) plus one node. On screen: a beat has a shape -- it starts somewhere and arrives somewhere -- instead of one clause held for 95 frames. Cost: zero downloads. Risk: exceeds the 77-token measured window per keyframe if not budgeted (the composer already measures with the SD1 tokenizer; reuse it); must add language, never damping words; the motif must survive in every keyframe or identity breaks. Judge: A/A null vs scheduled, on a writer-bank episode where intent fields are populated AND on a `scifi_news_pro` episode where only the shot description and palette exist.

4. **Speech-energy motion scaling.** Uses the per-beat master-mix slice that the render driver already attaches as `audio_ref` (a WAV sliced by the line's `start_s/dur_s`; the engine currently ignores it) to compute per-frame RMS/onsets (the same measures `audio_motion_profiles` stamps post-render, computed pre-render instead), fed as `ADE_ValueScheduling -> scale_multival` or `effect_multival`. **Scope note:** this is NOT a few hundred lines of Python only. The energy curve has to reach the engine's graph builder, and `VideoRequest` is `extra="forbid"` with `observability` documented "NEVER conditioning" -- so either the engine computes the curve itself from `audio_ref.path` inside `_build_render_request` (engine-local, no schema change) or a declared field is added to `VideoRequest` (a schema change, "a design item, not a mechanical one" per `eng_ghost_signal_official.py`). The engine-local route is the additive one. On screen: the picture stirs when the voice does -- a shouted line moves more than a whisper -- which is the radio-drama tie the current lane lacks entirely. Cost: zero downloads. Risk: multival extremes produce artefacts; clamp to a narrow band (e.g. 0.8-1.2) first. Judge: A/A null vs energy-scaled on a beat with a clear dynamic (the Tectal Echo beat 3, "Stability is stagnation ... we release it now").

5. **Context extras for long beats.** Uses `ADE_ContextExtras_ContextRef` (mode First, or Sliding) or `_NaiveReuse` on the existing static 16/4 context. On screen: a 95-frame beat spans EIGHT context windows today (stride 12: starts 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, and a final window at 79; the 125-frame opening bookend spans eleven) and drifts between them; ContextRef anchors later windows to the first, so a face that starts as Dr Zhang stays Dr Zhang. Pairs naturally with E1 (the first window is the still). Cost: zero downloads; more attention memory per window (measure the peak -- the 12B overnight ledgers already sit at 13.3-14.2 GB against the 14.5 GB target). Risk: over-anchoring re-introduces stillness. Judge: A/A null, then ContextRef First vs Sliding.

6. **FreeInit for temporal coherence.** `ADE_IterationOptsFreeInit` with 2-3 iterations through `ADE_AnimateDiffSamplingSettings.iteration_opts -> sample_settings`. On screen: less high-frequency flicker across frames -- and flicker is on record as a recipe/graph problem, which this addresses in the graph. Cost: zero downloads; sampling time multiplies by the iteration count. The dev-box baseline is the Tectal leg: 11 clips inside a 28:58 whole-episode run (the video phase alone is not timed on disk); the 24 GB pod's 2058 s / 8 clips is a different card and should not be extrapolated to the 5080. Expect the 5080 video phase to roughly double or triple; overnight profiles must budget for it. Risk: time only. Judge: A/A null vs FreeInit 2 vs 3.

7. **SparseCtrl RGB keyframes from the per-beat still.** The operator's named ask. Requires installing ComfyUI-Advanced-ControlNet (a new pinned pack in `otr_provision.py`) and downloading the v3 SparseCtrl RGB encoder (1.99 GB; scribble another 1.99 GB) from `guoyww/animatediff`. On screen: the still is a hard keyframe at frame 0 (and optionally the portrait-derived `scene_character` still at the last frame), with the motion module free in between -- the strongest identity-and-setting anchor SD1.5 AnimateDiff offers, and the published v3 pairing for exactly this module. Provenance: the still's `content_hash`, `prompt_hash`, `request_hash` and `derived_from_portrait_hash` rows already exist in `images.images[]`, so the keyframe can be cited by hash. Cost: one pack, 1-2 model files, extra VRAM for the control encoder (measure on the 5080 against the 14.5 GB target first, then the 4060). Risk: highest of the set -- a new pack pin, a new provisioning lane, a new missing-pack hint, and the 8 GB floor may not fit; run it on the dev box first and prove the 4060 unchanged before it touches any shipping profile. Judge as E1, plus a 4060 publish.

Two mechanisms, and they are NOT interchangeable on this module: (a) motion LoRAs via `ADE_AnimateDiffLoRALoader` into the Gen1 `motion_lora` socket -- additive to the graph, folder empty, download needed, but the original pan/zoom/tilt/roll LoRAs are v2-only per the ADE README (line 50) and would do nothing on `v3_sd15_mm` unless MotionDirector-trained v3 LoRAs are found; (b) CameraCtrl via `ADE_CameraPoseBasic` -- "specifically trained for v3 model" (README line 66-67), so it is the mechanism that fits this lane, but it is Gen2 only: `ADE_LoadAnimateDiffModelWithCameraCtrl -> ADE_ApplyAnimateDiffModelWithCameraCtrl -> ADE_UseEvolvedSampling` REPLACES `ADE_AnimateDiffLoaderGen1`, i.e. it changes the proven graph shape and is a separate, explicitly-approved arm rather than an addition. ## 8. WHAT IS OUT OF BOUNDS

- Trading any proven recipe cell for speed or VRAM; experiments add, they do not replace. A Gen2 loader swap (CameraCtrl, PIA, MotionCtrl, AnimateLCM-I2V) is a replacement and must be declared as its own arm.
- Fixing flicker or stillness by rewording prompts; the graph is where flicker lives.
- Damping words (`subtle`, `restrained`, `stable`, `gentle`) in any motion prompt, and any Python word-stripper as the fix.
- Story-quality work of any kind: prose, prompt-craft for better writing, writer-model benchmarking. (Adding a second LLM authoring pass for E3 would also be new prompt-craft; E3 stays deterministic.)
- A second shipping graph, a per-machine JSON, or an ad-hoc bench harness; `otr_canonical.json` plus saved dropdowns/profiles/variants is the only path.
- Reviving the tombstoned clean/v2/h3/h5 AnimateDiff ids; one haunted lane ships.
- Cloud or paid services, API keys, or any online dependency at render time.
- Moving, hiding or gating anything that publishes to `otr/obs/`.
- A change that alters the 4060's behaviour without a measured before/after on that box -- and the still prerequisite DOES alter it (Klein becomes gated for the lane).
- A shared-code fix asserted rather than measured on the other machine.

## 9. HOW SUCCESS IS MEASURED

An experiment counts only when its episode is published to `otr/obs/` by the canonical graph. The order is fixed: (1) the same-seed, same-ledger A/A null of the current recipe (at the E0-frozen adapter strength), published, so the noise floor is known; (2) the experimental arm on that seed, published beside it, with E9's durable prompt/seed record so the arm can be cited by hash; (3) the operator's eye, judging it as radio drama with pictures -- does the room hold, does the face hold, does the picture move with the voice, is it "overall better"; (4) no regression on the proven cells: the 8 GB class still publishes its leg (including the Klein-then-AnimateDiff combination the prerequisite introduces), the 5080 peak stays under 14.5 GB (measured against the 13.3-14.2 GB the lane already uses), the per-clip receipt still stamps the v3 recipe id (or a new, honestly-named one), and the frame-count invariant (`sum(render_frames - drop_head - trim_tail) == target_visible_frames`) still holds so no clip underruns its beat. Anything that improves the picture at the cost of one of those four is the wrong fix.
