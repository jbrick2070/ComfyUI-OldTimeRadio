## The rule, extrapolated from the ten (driver's reading -- to be confirmed by the operator)

1. **Draw what the line is ABOUT, not who is saying it.** Every character beat he touched
   replaced the speaker's costume with the SUBJECT of the dialogue: the reservoir, the
   organic matter drifting in it, the telemetry sensors and satellite signals, the paperwork.
   The picture is the story's world as the words describe it. People, when present, are
   "characters moving through" that world -- small, in it, never a coat in close-up.
2. **The episode lives in ONE place, and every beat is a view of it.** The reservoir recurs
   across his rewrites (he even names a real one, Williston Reservoir, BC, with driftwood).
   The lane today gives each beat a prop on a bare table; he gives each beat the setting.
3. **Motion is the world's motion.** Water drifting, organic matter moving with the current,
   signals coming down from the sky and hitting the water, driftwood floating, lights
   flashing -- not a figure tilting, turning a page, or stepping across a floor.
4. **The style is a wrapper said once**, at either end ("storybook engraving of ..." / "... in a
   storybook engraving style"), not a two-word prefix competing with a costume.
5. **The framing sentence is gone.** Not one rewrite carries "mid-shot or wider, whole figure
   legible, one clear action, unbroken shot" or "the object fills the frame on a table top".
   43% of every prompt today is that sentence.
6. **Radio objects stay on the announcer and music beds**, but placed in the setting (the
   radio set with the reservoir behind it), not on a table in a dark room.
7. **Fewer variables per prompt.** His follow-up, verbatim: *"maybe the ordering needs to be
   changed up, but the problem is you are adding too many variables to the scene and it's
   creating these often very similar shots of humans and bags etc."* Today a character beat
   carries build + coat colour + carried prop + a micro-action + framing; the object beats
   then draw that same carried prop (satchel, ledger, lantern) on a table -- which is why
   every episode converges on people and bags. One subject, one motion, the style. The prop
   cycle goes.

8. **Keep it simple: the visual style mixed with the CRUX of the story**, verbatim: *"we got to
   keep it real simple and think how do we mix the visual style with the crux of the subject
   in the story -- where even maybe if it's not being specifically mentioned in the beat."*
   The episode's central subject threads every beat, whether or not that beat's line names it.
9. **No costume or figure the dialogue never mentions**, verbatim: *"but definitely not some
   coat or figure that is not even mentioned in the dialogue -- unless the story is about a
   stolen coat and bag, then it's OK."* A prop or a garment earns its place only when the story
   is about it.

Where the words come from, in the ledger -- and they are ALREADY THERE: `meta.story_brief`
("A claustrophobic research station monitors a mysterious floating landmass that defies the
laws of physics." on this episode -- the crux, unused by the video lane today),
`meta.story_brief_terms` (setting: subsurface research station, remote reservoir, industrial
monitoring hub, isolated wilderness outpost; lighting; atmosphere -- the world, used only by
the plate), the beat's own spoken text (its nouns: pressure, water, temperature, telemetry,
superiors, paperwork), and the pack's language. NOT the cast look, NOT the prop pool.
