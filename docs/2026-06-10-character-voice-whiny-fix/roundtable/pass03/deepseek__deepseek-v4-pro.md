### 1. Blind Spots
The DOC is comprehensive and grounded, but there are a few plausible causes of a thin/pleading sound it misses entirely or doesn't weight heavily enough, considering the full chain.

*   **Reference-Clip Acoustics: Room Tone & Early Reflections.** The DOC correctly identifies RMS loudness (G18) as a primary mechanism. However, a zero-shot cloning model like IndexTTS2 can also clone the *acoustic space* of the reference. A donor recorded in a small, untreated room will have distinct early reflections and a boxy coloration, which when cloned, especially at a low effort level, can sound small, distant, and "thin" compared to a professionally voiced clip. Your `--audit` mode in P2a should include a metric for this. A simple proxy is the crest factor of the signal after a basic high-pass filter (e.g., 200 Hz), which can hint at the ratio of direct to reflected sound.
*   **Zero-Shot Cloner Behavior on Clean, Non-Speech Transients.** The DOC focuses on sustained vocal effort. A significant "whine" quality can come from the model's inability to handle clean stops and plosives (/p/, /t/, /k/) from a low-effort, quiet reference. If a reference has weak plosives, the zero-shot model may struggle to synthesize strong, percussive ones, leading to a breathy, unfocused, or "whining" sound on stressed syllables. This is a timbral/texture issue that an F0 or RMS measurement will miss. The "breath-tail ratio" is in P2a, which is good, but a measure of plosive energy (e.g., RMS of the first 10-20ms of a voiced segment following a stop) would be more direct.
*   **Seed Behavior is More Than Just Prosody Draw (G17).** You've identified per-line seed instability. A deeper blind spot: **for many TTS architectures, the deterministic seed of the vocoder or decoder can produce a subtly different "texture" or "noise profile" on the *same* prosodic contour**. A whiny line might not just be a nervous pitch contour, but could have a grainier, more aspirated sound baked into the spectral generation, purely due to RNG paths in the GAN/VAE decoder being seeded differently. A `fixed-character-seed` won't fix this, because the text is still different per line, so the RNG is consumed differently. The fix isn't a per-character seed, but potentially a *per-character, long-sequence noise schedule* (a wildcard for later). The P0 cell 8 test is essential but its result might be interpreted incorrectly. A "fixed seed is better" verdict might just mean the *text length happened to be more favorable to the RNG path*, not that prosody was better.

### 2. Craft
A voice director or audio post engineer would approach this differently, targeting the *perceptual* weight of the voice before touching the source generator.

*   **Targeted Parallel Compression for Vocal Weight.** A software solution that is completely automatable: after generation, and *before* mixing with the bed and announcer, apply parallel upward compression to the character's dialogue, but only to the 180-400 Hz (male) or 250-550 Hz (female) "body" band. Use a multi-band dynamics processor. The sidechain signal is the voice itself, band-passed to the target range. This band is heavily compressed (high ratio, fast attack/release) and blended back in with the original signal to taste. This adds a constant, "chesty" floor to the voice, mimicking vocal fold mass. It’s a standard post-production trick to add weight to thin voices and is trivially automatable as a `scipy.signal` or `sox` wrapper. This squarely addresses G15 (mix perception) but also the *quality* of the voice itself.
*   **The "Proximity Effect" EQ Curve.** As a first-pass filter, apply a low-shelf boost starting around 200 Hz (+3 to +6 dB) with a complementary wide-cut around 500 Hz (-2 dB). The proximity effect is a natural acoustic phenomenon where a sound source close to a directional mic has boosted low frequencies. Your ear is conditioned to associate this tonality with physical closeness, intimacy, and authority. Applying this `shelf` is a single IIR filter that is deterministic, additive, and zero-cost at render time. It doesn't change the TTS, it just re-voices the track like a mix engineer. This should be the first experiment in the "0/30/70/100% fixed?" offline test in P0a.
*   **Performance-Envelope Matching.** The `per-line` problem is a directorial one. An actor maintains a through-line. An automatable equivalent: take the winning `calm` or `angry` rollout from P0 cell 6, find the character's single line with the highest RMS and the lowest median F0 (i.e., their "strongest" line), and use its generated acoustic envelope as a formant-stable anchor. Then, very subtly cross-synthesize or morph the lower-energy lines toward this anchor's spectral tilt. This is highly speculative and complex, but is exactly the kind of craft fix a director would apply to a weak performance: "keep the energy of that take 3 we liked."
