VERDICT: Low craft level; the beat planner is the single biggest lever because it collapses every premise into the same control-room standoff template.

TOP FAILURE MODES:
1. Static standoff loop with no escalation or reversal. Every episode reduces to characters cycling through the same three actions (threaten lever, counter-threat, manual override). Evidence: Ep2 "Pull the lever or I'm tearing your terminal keys out of the dock"; Ep5 "If you put your hand on that manual bypass, Kenji, the frost is going to flood the hall"; Ep11 "You have three seconds to decide if you want to save the data or your lungs"; Ep17 "Hand over your thumbprint... or I'm hitting the scorched-earth switch". This fails as drama because tension never compounds or pays off; it just repeats. Root cause: beat planner produces 14-18 functionally identical "crisis at console" beats.

2. Dialogue consists almost entirely of procedural commands and ultimatums with zero subtext or character differentiation. Evidence: Ep1 "AI moderators, seize Charlie's droid"; Ep3 "My hand's on the abort lever now. You initial that waiver"; Ep8 "The boys aren't looking at their watches anymore, Chris; they're looking at your hands"; Ep14 "give me the clearance code or I'll have to let the pulse hit both our heads". This fails because the audience gets no interior stakes or relationships, only functional noise. Symptom of the line-composer receiving beats that are already pure action verbs.

3. Abrupt, unearned resolution via announcer tag instead of character decision. Evidence: Ep4 ends with "Starfall makes landfall on an uncharted island"; Ep6 "New Zealand's cave now holds its million-year-old fossils under locked glass"; Ep13 "tanning beds now bear warnings". This fails as drama because the story never reaches a dramatic choice or consequence. Root cause: beat planner never allocates beats for a decisive character action or reversal before the final tag.

4. Near-total loss of premise specificity once the control-room scene begins. Evidence: Ep6 (paleontology cave) produces the same lever threats as Ep2 (orbital rescue) and Ep17 (server farm); Ep13 (sun anomaly town) devolves into generic "containment breach" lines. This is cross-episode sameness generated by the beat planner overwriting premise details with its default template.

5. Severe under-length from early collapse into repetition. Multiple episodes (4, 9, 16) stop at 154-168 words because the line-composer exhausts its four reusable move types and has nowhere else to go.

HIGHEST-LEVERAGE FIXES:
1. In the beat planner, replace the generic 14-18 beat list with four required phases (Setup, Pressure, Reversal, Decision) and add an explicit rule forbidding more than two "lever/override/key" beats total. Smallest version: add two sentences to the planner prompt listing the four phases and the two-beat cap. Survives weak models because it is a hard structural constraint, not a style instruction.

2. In the beat planner, require at least one beat that forces a non-technical personal cost or relationship reveal before any override action. Smallest version: add one mandatory beat slot labeled "personal stake surfaces." Works on weak models because it is a required slot, not a quality nudge.

3. Move length enforcement from post-generation to the beat planner by mandating a minimum number of beats per phase (e.g., 3-4-3-4). This directly attacks the early collapse without needing a stronger writer model.

WON'T WORK / AVOID:
1. Adding more "avoid cliché / withhold objective / make it dramatic" sentences to the line-composer prompt. Weak models already ignore these.
2. Any QA/critic gate that only flags and rerolls without changing the beat planner; the model will regenerate the same template.
3. Post-generation editing passes that try to inject subtext; the underlying beats contain no material for subtext to be built from.

MODEL-vs-SYSTEM: The dominant sameness is pipeline-driven, not model-driven. Mistral-Nemo, Gemma, and the OpenRouter slot all produce the identical control-room standoff once the beats are set. Even the three episodes marked "strong" still follow the same template; the planner, not the writer, is the constraint.
