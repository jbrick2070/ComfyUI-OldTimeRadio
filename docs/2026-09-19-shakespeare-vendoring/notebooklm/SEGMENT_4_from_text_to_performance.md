# From text to performance: the ledger

## The one thing to take away (3-5 sentences)

A play file becomes a performance ledger when every speech has one machine-readable owner [nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:246-260, 431-474]. Source-grounded casting adds a second link from that owner to roster evidence; an unbound owner still speaks, but its voice falls back to the allocator [nodes/_otr_roster_gender.py:713-725; docs/OTR_STANDING_RULINGS.md:93-97]. The project calls that bridge a `speaker_map`: for example, the page label `1A STREGA` becomes the spoken Italian name `PRIMA STREGA`, which resolves gender through the English roster name `FIRST WITCH` [nodes/_otr_verbatim_corpus.py:583-593]. When one link is missing, the listener can hear the right words in the wrong mouth, the right character with a fallback voice, or no collective line at all [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:1253-1258; nodes/OTR_LedgerScriptWriter.py:3865-3874; nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:523-529]. The last case is the unresolved in-unison dilemma: the present runtime treats `ALL` as a collective that cannot own a cast slot, so it excludes the whole candidate passage containing that turn [nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:139-144, 273-279, 523-529].

## What we found (the contract, and the concrete failure each rule prevents)

The `speaker_map` keeps signed text untouched while storing printed label -> spoken name -> English roster [nodes/_otr_verbatim_corpus.py:583-590]. It may be absent, but a present map is refused unless its label, `spoken`, and `roster` values are nonblank [nodes/_otr_verbatim_corpus.py:603-650, 669-687]. `speaker_bindings` trims values and collapses label whitespace [nodes/_otr_verbatim_corpus.py:690-704; tests/test_verbatim_corpus.py:620-648].

`vendored_text` selects the language/play/scene row, reads UTF-8, verifies SHA-256, and returns text plus row; unreadable or changed bytes yield no vendored scene [nodes/_otr_verbatim_corpus.py:800-839]. The writer passes `speaker_bindings(_vendored_row)` into the planner, and the parser applies them before costing the passage [nodes/OTR_LedgerScriptWriter.py:3825-3842; nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:416-445]. Later, roster names become `resolve_as`, letting `PRIMA STREGA` keep her Italian name while `FIRST WITCH` supplies gender [nodes/OTR_LedgerScriptWriter.py:4286-4311; nodes/_otr_roster_gender.py:705-748].

The first failure is an invisible label: a colon cue needs a letter and, after `ª` and `º` are set aside as ordinals, no lowercase letter; otherwise its line joins the speech already open [nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:357-379, 453-473]. Hence “UPPER-CASE ON WRITE, ALWAYS”: on zero-based PDF indexes 40-41, printed pp.15-16, of Domingos Ramos's 1912 *Macbeth*, `ANGUS` and title-case `Ross` share the scene, and a stored `Ross` would merge his lines into the prior speaker [tmp/scan_cache/8802fae93d310a78061bb06c.pdf:PDF indexes 40-41; scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:1210-1215, 1253-1258; manifest row pt/macbeth/1.3]. This mixed-case example is edition-specific; the cited record does not establish a cross-edition title-case pattern [tmp/scan_cache/8802fae93d310a78061bb06c.pdf:PDF indexes 40-41; scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:1210-1215; manifest row pt/macbeth/1.3]. Separately, `_is_upper_label` ignores ordinal indicators before checking case; its regression fixture proves that labels such as `BRUJA 1.ª` and `1ª FAT` no longer vanish [tests/test_verbatim_corpus.py:1418-1454; nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:350-366].

The second failure is an owner with no roster binding, a case the ruling handles by preserving the printed label: an unbound cue costs a fallback voice, never a line [docs/OTR_STANDING_RULINGS.md:93-97]. The gender resolver tries increasingly loose sidecar-name matches and abstains on disagreement; unresolved names remain with the allocator [nodes/_otr_roster_gender.py:526-595, 705-725]. The writer warns because the words can stay correctly owned while the voice is wrong [nodes/OTR_LedgerScriptWriter.py:3865-3874]. Thus “Malvolio speaks with a woman's voice” is a correctness bug, while rewriting his dialogue is excluded quality work [CLAUDE.md:23-35].

Two forms strain the one-owner rule; the first is a song heading that names a singer without using a normal character cue [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:199-210]. Domingos Ramos's 1914 *A Tempestade* uses `Canto de Áriel` on zero-based PDF page index 51 instead of an `ARIEL` cue; before `18ffc540`, the song and chorus remained in Prospero's speech [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:199-205; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/RESULT_codex_batch_18ffc540.md:117-126; 18ffc540]. The fix turns a song word, preposition, and resolvable singer into an unspoken cue [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:208-210, 1217-1226]. Ramos's wording is edition-specific, but the device is a pattern: Jaime Clark's 1873 Spanish *Tempest* has `CANCION DE ARIEL.` on page index 33 [docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/RESULT_codex_batch_18ffc540.md:113-126; manifest row es/tempest/1.2].

The second form is several people speaking together, and the record shows it in unlike typography [config/source_banks/shakespeare/sources/macbeth__act1_scene3.txt:56-62; config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/pt/macbeth_1_3.txt:12; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/GROK_FOLD_TABLES_measured.md:67-87; config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/pt/tempest_1_2.txt:99]. Folger prints `ALL, [dancing in a circle]` before the witches' chant [config/source_banks/shakespeare/sources/macbeth__act1_scene3.txt:56-62]. Domingos Ramos's 1912 Portuguese *Macbeth* prints `TODAS TRES` on zero-based PDF page index 36 and maps it to roster `ALL` [config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/pt/macbeth_1_3.txt:12; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/PROMPT4_codex_break_the_coordinate_reader.md:30-33; manifest row pt/macbeth/1.3]. On zero-based PDF page index 249, Guillermo Macpherson's 1897 Spanish *Lear* prints the joint abbreviation `ALB . Y CORN.` inside the measured scene window 244-255; it remains honestly unbound because neither joint identity appears in that scene's sidecar [tmp/scan_cache/62fa124c89695cb646178c1b.pdf:PDF index 249; config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/leads.json:76-89; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/GROK_FOLD_TABLES_measured.md:67-87]. Ramos's 1914 *Tempest* instead prints `Côro (dispersamente)` inside Ariel's song on page index 51; because the stored line begins `ARIEL:`, the parser would assign that chorus text to Ariel whenever this speech is selected [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:199-205; config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/pt/tempest_1_2.txt:98-100; nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:431-474]. Together these are a cross-edition pattern: a collective may be a speaker, a translated phrase, a joint abbreviation, or text nested under a soloist [config/source_banks/shakespeare/sources/macbeth__act1_scene3.txt:56-62; config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/pt/macbeth_1_3.txt:12; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/GROK_FOLD_TABLES_measured.md:67-87; config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/pt/tempest_1_2.txt:99].

## Why it matters for a performance (what the listener hears when a link is missing)

A missed title-case cue lets the previous actor steal a speech; an unbound owner keeps the speech but rolls its voice; `ALL` makes the selector reject every window reaching that turn [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:1253-1258; docs/OTR_STANDING_RULINGS.md:93-97; nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:523-529]. The measured answer for `ALL` is therefore a **gap**, not one voice or a chosen group member [nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:139-144, 273-279, 523-529]. `Côro` would instead use Ariel's voice when selected, while the Macpherson joint remains a future unbound speaker because Spanish speaker emission was still open [config/source_banks/shakespeare/translations/pt/tempest_1_2.txt:99; nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:431-474; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/GROK_FOLD_TABLES_measured.md:25-33, 87; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/README.md:19-25]. The ledger decides which mouth exists for every line [nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:246-279; nodes/OTR_LedgerScriptWriter.py:4271-4311].

## For the hosts: three hooks (one line each) and two open questions

- **Hook:** A capital letter can decide who speaks: `Ross` looked fine on disk but could vanish as a cue and hand his words to the actor before him [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:1210-1215, 1253-1258].
- **Hook:** Ariel's song once belonged to Prospero because the printer wrote a song title where the machine expected a character label [scripts/otr_vendor_scan.py:199-205; 18ffc540].
- **Hook:** In the current ledger, `ALL` does not mean many voices; it means that passage cannot be selected [nodes/_otr_passage_selector.py:139-144, 523-529].
- **Open question:** Should a unison line choose one named member, use a dedicated chorus voice, or mix the members through a voice bus?
- **Open question:** Should the manifest record the members behind `TODAS TRES` and `ALB . Y CORN`, instead of reducing the first to `ALL` and leaving the second unbound [manifest row pt/macbeth/1.3; docs/2026-09-19-shakespeare-vendoring/GROK_FOLD_TABLES_measured.md:87]?
