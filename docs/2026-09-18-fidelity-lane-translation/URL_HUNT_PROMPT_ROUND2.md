## What qualifies as a hit — both, or leave it out

1. **You opened the source and read the scene's DIALOGUE.** A catalogue record,
   a library entry or a scan landing page is not a hit.
2. **Translated from Shakespeare's English**, not via a French or German
   intermediary. (Maffei's Italian *Macbeth* came from Schiller's German — that
   class is out.) Only reject on this if the edition itself says so.

Older is better where there is a choice, simply because older text is easier to
find freely — but do not spend time verifying dates.

## Columns

| lang | play | scene | translator | URL | edition's own scene label | speaker-label style | text or scan |

The two columns people get wrong:

- **edition's own scene label** — how *that edition* names it, verbatim
  (`SCENA III.`, `第三幕第一場`, `ESCENA PRIMERA`). Not the Folio's numbering.
  Editions renumber, and some drop acts entirely: Hugo's French Macbeth runs
  `SCÈNE I.`–`SCÈNE XXIV.` straight through with no act headings at all, so
  "Act 1 Scene 3" is not an address that exists in it.
- **text or scan** — native searchable text, or page images needing
  transcription.
