## What qualifies as a hit — all five, or leave it out

1. **You opened the source and read the scene's DIALOGUE.** A catalogue record,
   a library entry or a scan landing page is not a hit.
2. **Translated from Shakespeare's English**, not via a French or German
   intermediary. (Maffei's Italian *Macbeth* came from Schiller's German — that
   class is out.)
3. **Redistributable.** Wikisource, Wikimedia Commons, Internet Archive,
   Gutenberg, ePustakalay: fine. Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes:
   personal use only, forbids redistribution — not a hit no matter how good the
   text is.
4. **Translator died before 1956**, and you can point to a real record for the
   year — an authority file or encyclopaedia entry, not an inference from when
   their books stop appearing.
5. **First published before 1931**, or already public domain in its home
   country on 1996-01-01 and never previously published in the US.

## Columns

| lang | play | scene | translator (b–d) | death-year source | first pub | URL | edition's own scene label | speaker-label style | text or scan |

Two columns people get wrong:

- **edition's own scene label** — how *that edition* names it, verbatim
  (`SCENA III.`, `第三幕第一場`, `ESCENA PRIMERA`). Not the Folio's numbering.
  Editions renumber, and some drop acts entirely: Hugo's French Macbeth runs
  `SCÈNE I.`–`SCÈNE XXIV.` straight through with no act headings at all, so
  "Act 1 Scene 3" is not an address that exists in it.
- **text or scan** — native searchable text, or page images needing
  transcription.

## HINDI — one date carries seven scenes

**What year did Lala Sitaram die?** Born Ayodhya 1861. Translated *Bhul
Bhulaiya* (Comedy of Errors, 1915), *Denmark ka Rajkumar Hamlet* (1915), *Raja
Lear* (1915), *Jangal Mein Mangal* (Tempest, 1915), *Apni Apni Ruci* (As You

in **Hindi (लाला सीताराम)**; try National Library of India, Rare Book Society,
Hindi literary encyclopaedias.

He **renames characters and places to Indian ones. That is fine — not a reason
to exclude him.**
