# Deep-research prompt — round 2, the 32 remaining cells

Paste everything below the line. French, Italian and Mandarin are COMPLETE and

I need public-domain **Shakespeare translations** for a radio-drama project.
Four languages, 32 specific scenes. Please work through them in the priority
order below and report in the exact table format at the end.

## The one rule that decides whether an answer counts

**A bibliographic record, a library catalogue entry, or a scan landing page
does NOT count. You must actually open the source and reach the scene's
DIALOGUE.** If you located the right book but could not read the scene text,
say **NOT VERIFIED** and give me the URL anyway — that is useful and I will
not treat it as found. Do not infer that a scene exists because the book
exists.

## Four traps that have already cost me, so please check each one

1. **Indirect translations.** It must be translated **from Shakespeare's
   English**, not from a French or German intermediary. Andrea Maffei's
   Italian *Macbeth* had to be thrown out because his own preface says he
   worked from Schiller's German version. If the translator's preface names an
   intermediary, reject it and say so.
2. **Licence, not just copyright.** The text must be **redistributable**.
   Spain's Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes has an excellent Macpherson
   edition whose terms permit personal use only and forbid redistribution —
   unusable, even though the translation itself is public domain. Wikisource,
   Wikimedia Commons, Internet Archive and Project Gutenberg are fine. Quote
   the licence or PD tag you actually saw.
3. **Editions renumber scenes, and some have no acts at all.** François-Victor
   Hugo's French Macbeth runs `SCÈNE I.` through `SCÈNE XXIV.` continuously
   with **zero act headings**, so "Act 1 Scene 3" is not an address that exists
   in it. Tell me **how the edition itself labels the scene** (its own wording
   and numbering), not how the Folio does.
4. **Editions vs first publication.** A scan is often a later printing. Give me
   the **first publication year** of the translation AND the year of the
   edition you actually opened, separately, when they differ.

## What I need for every hit

- The **URL you opened** to read the dialogue.
- **Translator**, with **birth–death years**, and the **source for the death
  year** (a national-library authority record or an encyclopaedia entry, not an
  inference).
- **First publication year** of the translation.
- **Licence / PD tag** on the copy you opened.
- **How the edition labels the scene** (e.g. `SCENA III.`, `第三幕第一場`,
  `ESCENA PRIMERA`), and the **speaker-label style** (e.g. `MACBETH.`,
  `1ª Strega.`, `【歐】`).
- Whether it is **native searchable text** or a **page scan needing
  transcription**.

# PRIORITY 2 — Portuguese: one date unlocks four scenes

**The single highest-value question in this entire prompt:** what year did the
Portuguese Shakespeare translator **Domingos Ramos** die? He translated for
Lello & Irmãos in Porto from about 1908; the known editions include *Othello*
(1911), *Macbeth* (1912), *A Tempestade* (1914) and *Rei Lear* (1919).

Please check a **Biblioteca Nacional de Portugal authority record**, VIAF, or a
contemporary obituary. A secondary source claims 1914, but that appears to rest
only on his publications thinning out that year — and his own *Rei Lear* is
dated **1919**, which contradicts it. I need a real record, and "not found" is
a perfectly good answer.

If his death year turns out to be **before 1956**, four scenes unlock at once:
macbeth 1.3, tempest 1.2, tempest 3.1, king_lear 1.1.

**Then, seven Portuguese scenes with no lead at all:** twelfth_night 1.5,
twelfth_night 2.5, much_ado 2.3, much_ado 3.1, as_you_like_it 3.2,
romeo_juliet 2.2, comedy_errors 3.1.

The productive search here is **translators who died before 1889**, because
they clear every test outright without needing a date hunt. Two already work
for me: **António Feliciano de Castilho (1800–1875)** and **King Luís I of
Portugal (1838–1889)**. Did either translate any of those seven? Who else was
translating Shakespeare into Portuguese before 1890? Note that **Brazilian**
Portuguese translations are equally welcome.

Avoid **Henrique Braga** unless you can establish his death year — his
*Noite dos Reis* (1924) and *Muito barulho por nada* (1926) look tempting and
his 1955 editions are certainly too recent.

# PRIORITY 3 — Hindi: one date unlocks seven scenes

Same shape as Portuguese. **Lala Sitaram** translated a large series into
Hindi — *Bhul Bhulaiya* (Comedy of Errors, 1915), *Denmark ka Rajkumar Hamlet*
(1915), *Raja Lear* (1915), *Jangal Mein Mangal* (Tempest, 1915), *Apni Apni
Ruci* (As You Like It, 1917), *Macbeth* (1926), *Manmohan Ka Jaal* (Much Ado,
1926). One source says he was born in Ayodhya in 1861.

**What year did Lala Sitaram die?** Try a National Library of India or
Rare Book Society authority record, a Hindi literary encyclopaedia, or
Devanagari-language sources — searching in **Hindi (लाला सीताराम)** will
probably work better than English.

To be explicit, because it might look like a problem: Sitaram **renames the
characters and places to Indian ones**. That is fine for my purposes and is
not a reason to reject him.

Then, **scan URLs** for his volumes — I have one already at
`epustakalay.com/book/67288-bhool-bhulaiya-by-shri-sitaram/`. Try ePustakalay,
archive.org (the DLI collection), and Rekhta.

**Six Hindi scenes have no Sitaram lead at all:** tempest 3.1, midsummer 3.1,
midsummer 3.2, twelfth_night 1.5, twelfth_night 2.5, romeo_juliet 2.2. Did he
translate *A Midsummer Night's Dream*, *Twelfth Night* or *Romeo and Juliet*?
If not, who else was translating Shakespeare into Hindi before 1931?

and **José Arnaldo Márquez (1832–1903)** elsewhere and either would be ideal
if they translated this play.
