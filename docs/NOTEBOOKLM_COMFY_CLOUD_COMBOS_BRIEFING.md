# Signal Lost on Comfy Cloud: the costume, not the station

A source briefing for a NotebookLM audio overview aimed at curious listeners and Signal Lost fans. Measured 2026-09-15/16 from the shipping graphs and the live watch folder. This is not a build log. It is what the show *looks and sounds like* when the same radio play puts on a paid costume.

Upload this file (and, if you want the earlier eras, `NOTEBOOKLM_SIGNAL_LOST_ERA_BRIEFING.md`) into NotebookLM. Ask for an Audio Overview. Keep the hosts off process and on pictures, voices, and the odd combinations.

## The station stays home

Signal Lost is still a local radio play. The script has a start, a middle, and an end. The announcer still opens the show. The voices still belong to named people. The finished file still has to land in the watch folder or it did not happen.

What Comfy Cloud changes is the *wardrobe*:

- the stills can be Luma Photon Flash instead of a card GPU drawing a face
- the moving picture can be Vidu or LTX 2.5 instead of a green CRT scope
- the voices can be ElevenLabs instead of Kokoro
- the bed can be Sonilo instead of Stable Audio
- the writer can be a hosted model billed through Comfy Credits instead of Gemma living in VRAM

The pack does not move onto hosted Comfy Cloud as a foreign app. The station is still the machine in the room. It phones partners for costumes, then it cuts the episode the way it always has: captions, credits, the file in `otr/obs`.

If you only remember one sentence: **the play is local; the glamour is rented.**

---

## Why this combo is strange (and why that is the point)

Most Comfy Cloud demos are one pretty clip. Signal Lost is a *broadcast*:

1. Someone writes a radio play.
2. Cast gets voices that do not swap gender mid-show.
3. Music plays under and between.
4. Each beat gets a first frame, then a moving picture that is supposed to hold that face.
5. The pictures are cut to the audio, not the other way around.
6. The title card in the watch folder is how a human knows it worked.

That is a lot of rented glamour that has to agree with one another. The devil is in those agreements, not in the brand names.

## The look they almost never rolled -- recursive fractal light field

In the older archive, recursive fractal is the wallflower. One coded news episode, *Magnetic Pulse*, wore it, and it arrived huge. Tonight they pinned it on purpose, on My Story, on the cloud costume.

What the style actually asks the picture to be:

- laser cyan, spectral magenta, acid green, ultraviolet blue, molten amber
- deep graphite black, not studio gray
- nested dials inside dials, speaker grilles that become mouths, raster lattices that breathe
- the announcer as an appliance with a face: two glass tuning-dial eyes, a radiant vector mouth, cabinet filling the frame
- portraits with readable lips and controlled bloom, not a wash of tags

If Vidu or LTX is handed a *brown box* as the first frame, the moving picture stays a brown box with a little camera shrug. If it is handed a Luma still of flared radio tubes, the clip looks like the old Vidu episodes in the watch folder -- *The Bird of Dawning*, *Homecoming Orbit*, *The Christening Gown*. The first frame is the costume. The video model is the walk.

## My Story on that light field: Stomp, Tiptoe, and the cat

My Story is the listener's own idea, not a scraped feed. When nobody types one, the house idea is a toy playground:

Two small orange toy boots, Stomp and Tiptoe, play with a tennis ball beside a hand-high slide and a white picket fence. Stomp is loud and kicks first. Tiptoe counts to three. They only agree on three. Whiskers, a little toy cat, talks when he wants to, usually last. Rainbow smoke. Three plastic boxes, red green blue. A toy hairdryer that whirrs by itself. A candy bar bigger than both of them. A humming rainbow candy. They melt chocolate with the hairdryer's breath because the play stove is painted fire. They set out one bowl too many. Whiskers says that bowl is his.

The cheap cloud 1-act asks for three speakers. Deluxe asks for four. A house idea that told the cat never to speak used to fight that cast: the last act would refuse until Whiskers had a line, then the cleaner would spend Credits repairing statue-talk. The spec now lets him speak.

Opening flavor, already proven on the local My Story 1-act *The Count of Three*: "Kick it! Kick it now!" / "Wait. We haven't counted yet. One... two... three. Now."

## Three costumes on the same play

### Cheap cloud -- the daily dress

Vidu Q2 Pro Fast at 720p for every role: announcer, music, people. Luma Photon Flash for the still that Vidu has to hold. ElevenLabs for the throats. Sonilo for the orchestra. Sonnet 5 writes; Luna keeps the ledger honest.

This is the costume that already produced a full 1-act in about twenty minutes when every clip was requested together and then laid back in show order. It is not "one video." It is a handful of rented shots that must not shuffle the story.

### Deluxe Foley -- the picture that also hums

Same Luma stills, same ElevenLabs, same Sonilo. The writer steps up: GPT 5.6 Sol writes, Luna still does the books. Four people in the cast. Seven acts in the long graph; they have been proving it at one act first.

The moving picture is LTX 2.5 image-to-video with its own native bed turned on. That bed is not the voices. The voices are still the radio play. The bed is harvested, the picture is stripped silent, and the bed is mixed half-and-half under the master -- the same mix the local Foley lane has used for months. You should hear the room in the picture *and* the people, neither eating the other.

If the Foley clip is mute, it is a failure, not a style. If the picture is bland, look at the still, not the mixer.

### Deluxe audio-in -- the mix drives the mouth

Same Sol, Luna, Luma, ElevenLabs, Sonilo, four people. The picture is LTX 2.5 audio-to-video. The episode mix is the clock: the clip's length is the sound's length. Character beats own a human mouth. The still is a wide scene, not a talking-head close-up. This is not Foley. Foley adds a bed under voices. Audio-in lets the voices steer the face.

Two deluxe costumes exist because those are two different kinds of magic, and putting both on one graph would lie about which one you are watching.

## The writers as a cast, not a stack

Cheap cloud: Sonnet 5 is the playwright. Luna is the script supervisor who will not let a name change sex between beats.

Deluxe: Sol is the playwright. Luna still sits in the booth with the stopwatch.

They are billed through Comfy Credits -- the same account that pays Vidu and Luma -- not a second tab of API keys. The local writer (Gemma on the 16 GB box, Qwen on the small cards) is still what the show was built on. Cloud writing is a guest star, not a replacement of the station.

Luna is told not to sit and think. Sonnet cannot fully stop thinking, so it thinks a little. Sol is allowed the catalog default. That is why a deluxe 1-act can feel slower in the writing booth than a cheap one, even before any picture is rented.

## Clips that arrive late, in the wrong order, on purpose

A local card renders beats one after another because it only has one pair of hands.

Cloud partners can be asked all at once. The shots land when they land. The danger is obvious and cinematic: the last line of the act arriving first, the announcer's close playing in the middle, a face from scene two glued to scene one's voice.

The rule they actually use: ask for every cloud shot together, remember the show order, and only then cut. First-to-last-frame tricks that need two stills in one request do not get this treatment. The cheap Vidu lane is first-frame only, which is why the Luma still matters so much.

A 1-act in this costume has already finished in about twenty minutes when the fan-out held. That is the "it can zoom" number. It is still not instant. Writing the play, minting stills, and waiting on partners is the show, not a spinner.

## Challenges, said as pictures

**The first frame is the episode.** A tag soup ("cinematic, 8k, masterpiece") washes LTX into a postcard. A long sentence with camera motion and a real still does not.

**The bed and the voices share one speaker.** Foley at half-and-half is a radio choice: the room is present, the play is still the play. Mime would throw the voices away. Audio-in would let the voices puppet the face. Mixing those up is how you get a talking statue or a silent movie with a jazz record glued on.

**Sequence is a moral problem.** If clip three arrives first and you concatenate in arrival order, the story lies. Fans will hear it immediately.

**Credits are not infinite glamour.** A 1-act can still spend a long time in the writing booth with Sol and Luna arguing over every line of the ledger. The cheap costume is the one that already made a whole show. Deluxe is the one they are proving now, one act at a time, same My Story, same fractal light.

**The hosted gallery will not run this pack.** Curious Comfy people will look for Old Time Radio on the Cloud node list and not find it. The fans already know the station is a folder of episodes. Both audiences need the same sentence: the play is built here; Cloud is the costume shop.

**Fans.** You already wait for the watch folder. Do not tell either audience about sprints. Tell them about the count of three, the extra bowl, the dials inside dials, and the first frame that decides whether the walk is a radio play or a brown box.
