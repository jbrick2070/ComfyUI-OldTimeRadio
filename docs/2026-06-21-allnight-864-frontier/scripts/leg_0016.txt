# A Column Called Nothing

Tonight, beneath the parched canopies of Costa Rica, one patient observer keeps watch as a fractured troop, a cruel drought, and a single impossible record test all we thought we knew.
Morning, Rainn - coffee's gone cold again. They came down to the river an hour before light, all of them, and not one touched the figs. Write it slow, will you, every name in order. Something's turned, and I want it on paper before we start guessing.
...Thirty years these pages say they feed at noon, never the water before sun. Same ink, same hand. I underlined it twice, back when I thought a thing could stay underlined.
The figs are still on the branches, Allan - heavy, splitting in the heat, and they walked right past them to the mud. Maybe the river's not the new thing. Maybe it's the last thing left to teach them when everything they knew dried up under our underlining.
Put the pen down a moment - no, leave it open, I want the page to stay open. Thirty years I logged them by name, and not one season did the order break. They don't forget themselves in a dry summer, Nia. They wait. They've always waited, and so have I.
Musical interlude bridging setup into the next phase.
I counted nine at the water before light, Allan, and here's the tally beside your noon column for the same nine - your own hand says one thing, my chalk says another, and I can't make them agree no matter how I hold the page to the lamp.
The river's down to the bones this year, that's all - give it one good rain and they'll be back under the figs by midday, in order, the way they've kept it. A dry season bends them. It doesn't unwrite thirty years.
Then explain the wet weeks in March, when the figs were swollen and they still went down at dawn - that's not the drought talking, that's three columns now, all leaning the same way, and you keep handing me the rain like it covers a hole I've already measured.
I have a box of these in the cellar - 'ninety-four, 'ninety-seven, the spring the wells ran sweet - and there are mornings in my own margins where the dawn count won't sit still either, and I wrote "transcription error" in pencil so I could rub it out before anyone held the page to a lamp.
Pencil, was it - soft enough to lift, easy enough to forget you ever pressed it down. Tell me, when you rubbed it out, were you correcting the page, Nia, or the part of you that already knew, and needed the whole edifice to keep standing under your name?
Mind that open page of yours before you tally mine - March of the swollen figs is right there in your own column, and you skipped it clean, didn't you, left a tidy blank where the dawn count should've gone, so don't hand me the rain and keep the gap for yourself.
Musical interlude bridging complication into the next phase.
Yes - I left it blank, and I'll tell you why I couldn't bring myself to write a number there: my hand knew the figs had gone down at dawn, and a blank was the one honest thing I had, because the truth was they'd started doing something the old column had no box for.
Then hand me the eraser, Nia, and the pencil with it - I'll not rub a thing out tonight, I'll rule a fresh column beside the old one and head it nothing, just leave the width for whatever they decide to become, and let the lamp find both.
Hand it here and I'll widen it myself - remember the first season, Rainn, when we drew the cage before we'd ever met the creature, and called every fig that fell outside it a mistake of ours instead of a choice of theirs.
Slide the lamp this way, then - every box I ever ruled was just the floor someone had to stand on to see over the wall, and tonight I'll initial it and date it for whoever comes after us, so they know the blank was left open on purpose, not left undone.
And so the watch continues beneath those changing canopies, where UCLA's patient record-keepers in Costa Rica still measure how a warming world reshapes the quiet societies of the white-faced capuchin.
