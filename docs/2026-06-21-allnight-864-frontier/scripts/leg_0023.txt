# The Port Jaw Holds

Tonight, we follow a daring startup racing gravity itself, as Swift waits in the dark for one precise latch and a saving burn.
If Swift burns up before dawn, Miss Gordon, so does every promise we sold to keep the doors open; I need you to tell me whether there's still sky enough to catch her.
She's worth catching because her core's still talking, but not loudly, and that cracked gyro won't forgive a rough hand; we've got maybe two clean passes before the atmosphere starts collecting its debt.
The men with the ledgers won't wait for sunrise, Dale; if we miss Swift, they'll call it proof we never belonged up there, and if we touch her wrong, the sky will do their arguing for them.
Then let them sharpen their pencils; we're going after our ship because we sent her, and because a company that can't bring back its own bird doesn't deserve wings-Anya, tell me where this plan breaks.

The interceptor was taught to shake hands with a steady lady, not chase Swift while she's rolling end over end; unless Kevin rewrites her guidance before the next pass, we either clip a wing or watch her go.
Then I'll make her less polite. We slave the jets to ground timing, cut the safeties down to the bone, and fly the match from here until the latch sees metal.
Kevin, that pencil in your hand is snapped clean through; tell me the part where this doesn't spend our last payroll, blow the bureau's clock, and still leave us reaching for a ship that's tumbling faster by the minute.
The payroll's already in orbit, Dale; my trick is to let the rescue bird think with her ankles-ground pulses straight into the jet box-but that means the guidance stack takes loads nobody signed its name to, and Anya gets to choose whether it burns hot for ninety seconds or Swift burns alone.
No, Kevin, I get to choose after you show me numbers that last longer than your cigarette ash; Dale takes the jaws, you take the brain, and if either one of you says "probably," we wave at Swift on the next pass and call the newspapers liars.
I'll own the jaws; I ordered them, I blessed them, and I'll stand under them if they come down-but the port actuator's been stuttering since Thursday, and if Kevin can't make that kiss softer than a dock rope, the grapple shears the first time it tastes metal.

Dale can quit measuring coffin boards; the spike kissed sixty-two percent of his margin and laid down flat-your stuttering port jaw held like it had manners, Anya, and I've still got half a snapped pencil to do the subtraction.
Then don't spend it writing apologies; Swift's not falling anymore... and if anyone asks what this little shop does, tell them we reach up with greasy hands and bring our own home.
Bring her home, yes... after Maribel's third shift, and Joe's bad weld cut out twice, and Kevin sweet-talking a port actuator that still owes me an honest night's sleep; we got lucky where the work let us.
Leave the lights on in the bay, Anya-if Swift can keep looking for us after this, maybe there's room up there for outfits small enough to know every scar on their own machinery.
And so the chase goes on, with Swift falling and a young firm reaching upward; whether rescue finds its mark, only time will tell.
