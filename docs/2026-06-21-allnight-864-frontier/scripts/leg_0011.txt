# The Accusing Tooth

Good evening, listeners; tonight we follow a first cough in Siberia, where old teeth whisper what the graves would not.
Five thousand five hundred years under Siberian ice, and not a voice out of that burial ground... till one old tooth turns up, like it had been waiting to accuse somebody.
Accusations can wait, Lemmy. That tooth is evidence-fragile as chalk-and if the DNA holds, it may tell us whether plague was already moving through those people before history learned its name.
Fragile, yes... but it sat in a mouth that knew why the fires went cold in that Siberian camp, and I keep turning it in this cotton like it might still decide whether to speak.
Then let Oxford ask it properly, Zuri. If plague was there before the chronicles had ink for it, that little tooth may be the first witness that doesn't flinch.

Oxford can ask, but it may not get a clean answer... the plague markers are there and not there, broken at the edges, mixed with modern breath enough to make a saint look guilty.
Then that tooth is sitting on a knife-blade, Kearney; one side says we've moved the shadow of plague back before the old maps, the other says we've put our name to a dirty tube.
The extraction log has a blank where no blank can be... forty minutes unaccounted for, and Lemmy's got to swear the chain held while our best read turns to smoke in his hands.
I can carry it from trench to tray to the van, Zuri... every hand, every seal; but the field bag's got grit under the tape where there shouldn't be grit, and that was before Oxford ever breathed on it.
Then perhaps Oxford isn't the sinner we need. Perhaps the ground taught the tooth a false prayer long before we called it evidence.
If the graveyard can speak in borrowed voices, we either polish the story till it shines clean... or we let it crack and watch the whole plague road move under our feet.

Then we stop calling it damage and start calling it witness... if the tooth points farther back, we move the road, not the truth.
And we don't smile while doing it; those people vanished without names, Kearney... the least we owe them is to let the record lose its comfort.
Then write it with the hush it deserves... not as a date shifted in a ledger, but as a stone set down for every poor soul who had only fever for a name.
Aye... and mind the tooth when you set it there, Mister Scott. Small thing, but it's carried their last word farther than any bell ever could.
So closes our account: from Siberia's ancient teeth, the oldest sequenced plague tells us the scourge shadowed wanderers before towns ever crowded the map.
