# Twelve Degrees Off

Tonight, NASA wagers on daring hands of metal, racing to catch Swift before the faithful eye in the heavens goes dark.
Swift's old, Dale, but she still catches fire in the dark better than half the new glass we brag about; if we wait for the polite answer, she'll be gone, so NASA's listening to long shots with their hats in their hands.
Then we'll stop asking the model to sing for its supper and put metal on the table-capture, couple, boost-before that little clock of yours eats the last margin.
NASA isn't afraid you'll miss Swift, Gus; they're afraid you'll touch her-put a bright new hand on an old bird and leave fingerprints no apology can polish off.
Then we don't ask them for faith; we give them loads, tolerances, cold runs, failed runs, every scar before flight... and we do it while that clock on Dale's table keeps shaving us down.
Musical interlude bridging setup into the next phase.
The window just got meaner-Swift drops through Earth's shadow on the second pass, not the third, and NASA wants a live review board watching our capture proof by Friday; that little clock hand isn't moving now, it's twitching.
Friday's a fine day for coffee and funerals, Dale; what I haven't heard is whether that latch-and-lift knows the difference between a bird holding still for inspection and Swift coming in crooked with forty years of bad habits.
In the clean room she'll kiss the fixture like she was born to it... but Swift isn't a fixture, and if we don't say that out loud before NASA hears the motor hum, they'll think the silence was part of the design.
Call it candor if you like, Gus, but if we stroll into Friday saying our hands are clean and our latch is guessing, NASA won't hear engineering-they'll hear a calendar begging for a mercy killing; so either we tell them before they ask, or we show Swift can come in ugly and still be caught.
Then we make the clean room ugly by dawn; I'll put the review risk on my badge, run the latch off-nominal, let it scrape every assumption we've been polite about, and if it behaves, Friday still has a pulse.
Dale... that clock on your table just stopped sounding like a warning and started sounding like a judge; run it, but understand-if the latch blinks in there, our wager dies on the floor, not over Earth.

Gus, your line stays where you drew it; but the sim has already swallowed the crooked approach, the lag, the dead-band, and still found the throat-Dale, put the number on the table.
She came in twelve degrees off, the jaws took her on the first bite, held through the lift profile, and raised Swift clear; if Friday wants blood, it won't get it from the latch.
Twelve degrees off and she still found home... all right. Then this stops being my wager or Dale's badge; from here on, nobody spends courage faster than procedure can count it.
Turn that clock face-down, will you? Swift gets her ride, this room gets its answer, and Friday gets men and women careful enough to make a wild bet look earned.
And so NASA's clock is running, while Swift descends toward its appointment with three metal arms and an answer still hidden above.
