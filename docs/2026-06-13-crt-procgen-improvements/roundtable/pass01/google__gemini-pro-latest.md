1. **Vertical-Hold Loss (V-Sync Roll) on Dead Air**
   *Why it fits:* The ultimate broadcast-dread artifact; literally "losing the signal" during silent scene gaps. 
   *Draw approach:* In render() section 8, when `_brightness_ema` drops below a threshold (e.g., 0.1), apply a modulo Y-offset to the `_scanlines` overlay paste and/or shift the base image array vertically, wrapping top-to-bottom. 
   *Cost:* Cheap (array `np.roll` or PIL `paste` offset).

2. **Phosphor Bloom/Overdrive on Loud Transients**
   *Why it fits:* Simulates physical CRT electron-beam spread when pushed past normal voltage, making loud noises feel physically aggressive to the hardware.
   *Draw approach:* In section 2/5 (the rings), if per-frame `vol` spikes significantly above `_brightness_ema` (e.g., > 2.0x), draw a second, slightly scaled-up iteration of the ring/waveform with a thicker line width (4-6px) and low brightness (e.g., G=50).
   *Cost:* Medium (extra Pillow draw loop per spike).

   *Cost:* Cheap (scalar math on existing numpy array).

AESTHETIC RISKS / TRAPS

*   **The Green-Only Luma Trap:** Code uses `CRT_AMBER (255, 176, 0)` and `CRT_CYAN (0, 200, 200)`. Because downstream is a GREEN-ONLY `screen` blend, Amber will evaluate purely as `176` (dimmer than Cyan's `200`). Attempting to use these constants for "flashes" will result in confusing, dull luma drops. *Every accent must be defined purely by its Green channel value (0-255).*
*   **Line-Weight Clutter:** Drawing the oscilloscope or FFT with `width > 2` will make it look like flat vector UI or an EQ plugin. Authentic CRT traces are hairline-thin and achieve "thickness" via brightness/bloom, not geometry width. Keep `draw.line` at `width=1` or `2`.
*   **Smooth-Motion Teletype:** Tying the title teletype reveal to a pure float `t` might result in half-rendered frames or anti-aliasing mush. The teletype must step on integer frame intervals (e.g., update 1 char every 2 frames) to retain a rigid, mechanical feel.
*   **Landscape Edge-Bleed (The Open Decision):** If rings are pushed to the far edges of 1472x832 landscape b-roll, they risk being cropped by TV overscan or fighting with the subject if the LTX b-roll is framed wide. They must have a hard inner-bound limit (e.g., `x > 150` and `x < w - 150`).
